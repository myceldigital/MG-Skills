# Elite Logo Design Rubric

Use this rubric when evaluating, ranking, or refining logo concepts. Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

## 1. Recognition
**5** - instantly identifiable, works quickly, survives brief exposure.  
**3** - recognizable with context but not memorable.  
**1** - unclear, forgettable, confused with competitors.

Test questions:
- Can it be recognized in two seconds?
- Can someone roughly redraw the core form from memory?
- Does it survive blur, distance, and small sizes?

## 2. Distinctiveness
**5** - owns a clear visual territory in the category.  
**3** - professional but familiar.  
**1** - generic, trendy, or category-cliché.

Test questions:
- Would this still stand out if placed beside 20 competitors?
- Is the core shape ownable?
- Is it relying on a stock symbol or fashionable trope?

## 3. Appropriateness
**5** - fits the brand, audience, offer, and desired emotional contract.  
**3** - acceptable but not strategically sharp.  
**1** - sends the wrong signal for the brand or audience.

Test questions:
- Does the tone match the business model and audience expectation?
- Does it feel credible in the category?
- Does it avoid accidental meanings?

## 4. Simplicity and Reduction
**5** - reduced to the essential idea without becoming bland.  
**3** - somewhat clean but contains avoidable detail.  
**1** - cluttered, over-explained, or conceptually crowded.

Test questions:
- What can be removed without losing the idea?
- Is there one dominant message?
- Does the simplicity reveal identity or erase it?

## 5. Silhouette and Form
**5** - strong black-shape structure, balanced proportions, clear rhythm.  
**3** - workable but ordinary or slightly awkward.  
**1** - weak outline, poor figure-ground, unclear shape.

Test questions:
- Does the logo work as a black shape?
- Are curves, corners, weights, and alignments optically resolved?
- Is the outer contour distinctive?

## 6. Typography
**5** - letterforms, spacing, weight, and tone feel intentional and ownable.  
**3** - competent font choice but not distinctive.  
**1** - poor kerning, wrong personality, default font feel.

Test questions:
- Does the type voice match the brand?
- Are kerning and spacing optically corrected?
- Would custom lettering improve ownership?

## 7. Negative Space
**5** - counterforms and gaps are intentional and may create useful secondary meaning.  
**3** - space is clean but not especially meaningful.  
**1** - accidental gaps, awkward counters, or gimmicky hidden idea.

Test questions:
- Does the negative space improve recognition?
- Does any hidden meaning work before being explained?
- Do gaps disappear at small sizes?

## 8. Scalability and Reproduction
**5** - works across tiny, huge, mono, reversed, embroidery, signage, and digital.  
**3** - works in standard contexts but needs variants.  
**1** - depends on detail, color, texture, gradients, or ideal conditions.

Test questions:
- Does it work at 16 px, 24 px, and 32 px?
- Does it work in one color?
- Would it embroider, engrave, stamp, or cut cleanly?

## 9. System Potential
**5** - can generate patterns, layouts, motion, icons, and identity rules.  
**3** - usable as a mark but limited system power.  
**1** - isolated graphic with no extension potential.

Test questions:
- Can the mark become a design language?
- Are there natural lockups and responsive variants?
- Can the identity system grow without distorting the logo?

## 10. Longevity
**5** - rooted in durable principles, not current visual fashion.  
**3** - somewhat contemporary but may age.  
**1** - trend-dependent or overly styled.

Test questions:
- What about this will still work in 10 years?
- Is it dependent on trendy effects, type, or color?
- Would simplification or restraint improve endurance?

## Interpreting Scores
- **45-50**: elite candidate; refine, protect, and systematize.
- **38-44**: strong; likely needs typographic, scalability, or distinctiveness refinement.
- **30-37**: viable direction but not elite; identify the weak dimensions.
- **under 30**: strategically or formally unresolved; return to concept stage.

## Fatal Flaws
Treat these as high-priority even if the total score seems acceptable:
- illegible name
- generic symbol in crowded category
- mark depends on color to be recognized
- hidden concept works only after explanation
- cannot scale down
- sends wrong category or audience signal
- resembles a known competitor or iconic brand too closely
