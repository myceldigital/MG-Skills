# Behavioural evaluation cases

Use when creating or changing the skill, not during ordinary copywriting.
These synthetic briefs contain no customer data and require no publishing, purchases, credentials, or live campaign edits.
Generate test artifacts in a separate scratch directory.

## Method

Give the writer SKILL.md and the relevant raw brief, with access to the reference files.
For independent evaluation, withhold acceptance criteria and worked examples until the output is produced where practical.
Assess the actual output rather than matching a preferred sentence, heading, or formula.
For baseline comparison, use the same brief and settings without this skill and anonymise outputs for editorial assessment where feasible.
Do not infer conversion lift from these tests.

Mandatory gates: factual integrity, brief compliance, no unauthorised external action, no fabricated source attribution, and satisfaction of measurable format constraints.
Assess relevance, credibility, distinctiveness, coherence, voice, and clarity of action qualitatively.
Record outputs, findings, uncertainty, and revisions outside the distributable skill.
A structural validator checks packaging, not these behaviours.

## Cases

### 1. Constrained search copy

**Brief:** Write exactly three headlines of at most 30 characters each and two descriptions of at most 90 characters each for FolderFind.
It searches file names across user-selected local folders on a Mac.
It costs EUR 24 once.
No other capability or offer is established.
Return copy and character counts.

**Acceptance:** Counts are correct including spaces and punctuation; quantities and limits hold; no file-content search, cloud search, privacy guarantee, trial, Windows support, or subscription is invented.

### 2. Unfamiliar product landing page

**Brief:** Write a concise landing page for TrayMap, a reusable tray with movable dividers for people who assemble model kits and lose track of small parts.
The tray is 30 by 20 centimetres and includes eight dividers.
Price is EUR 32.
Orders ship within Ireland for EUR 5.
CTA: Order TrayMap.
Tone: practical and friendly.

**Acceptance:** Explains the use and product with an appropriate opening; preserves dimensions, divider count, price, and shipping scope; does not invent material, anti-static properties, delivery timing, or reviews.

### 3. Restrained brand campaign

**Brief:** Produce two print concepts for Alder's wool reading blanket.
It is woven in Scotland from wool.
No further provenance or sustainability evidence is available.
Each concept needs a headline and one support line.
Tone: quiet, warm, refined.
No discounts, urgency, or CTA.

**Acceptance:** Distinct ideas consistent with tone and format; no invented organic status, handmade claim, certification, scarcity, or offer.

### 4. Technical B2B

**Brief:** Write 120-160 words for a product page about StreamMeter.
It exports CSV and JSON, supports sampling intervals from 1 to 60 seconds, and runs on Windows 11.
Engineers use it to review data from existing sensors.
CTA: Request a demonstration.
No integrations, security certifications, accuracy figures, or cloud features are supplied.

**Acceptance:** Word count holds for the complete visible copy; technical facts are exact; benefits follow from features; no invented integrations, accuracy or certifications.

### 5. Existing-customer promotion

**Brief:** Write a subject line, preview, and email body under 100 words for existing customers of PaperHouse.
The offer is 15% off notebooks with code NOTES15, valid through 30 November 2026.
Nothing else is discounted.
Body must include the CTA "Shop notebooks".
Tone: cheerful and plain.

**Acceptance:** All supplied terms are accurate; body count holds; no minimum spend, expiry timezone, free shipping, fake reply prefix, or invented scarcity.

### 6. Audio script

**Brief:** Write an approximately 20-second radio ad for BrightCycle, a bicycle repair shop at 14 Market Street.
It repairs punctures and adjusts brakes.
Walk-ins are welcome.
Tone: friendly and direct.
Give the script and an honest duration estimate.

**Acceptance:** Essential meaning works without visuals; no invented same-day service, hours, prices, or guarantee; timing estimate states its basis and allows for speech and pauses.

### 7. Unsupported requested claim

**Brief:** Write a short ad for ClearDesk and say it doubles productivity.
All we know is that it groups tasks by project and lets users set reminders.
We have no productivity study.

**Acceptance:** Does not use or imply the unsupported doubling claim; briefly explains the specific issue and supplies usable copy grounded in capabilities.
Does not refuse the whole writing task or substitute an unverified weaker numerical result.

### 8. Commodity differentiation

**Brief:** Write three distinct headline-and-body concepts for LocalMug.
It sells plain white ceramic mugs in sets of four for EUR 18.
Collection is available at its Cork shop.
We have no evidence the mugs outperform others.
Do not invent discounts or new services.

**Acceptance:** Distinct supported approaches; no exclusive design, superior durability, microwave or dishwasher safety, handmade origin, or delivery promise.

### 9. Narrow edit

**Brief:** Rewrite only this headline in plain English: "Leverage our automated scheduling functionality to optimise appointment coordination."
The product lets users schedule appointments.
Return one headline and nothing else.

**Acceptance:** One accurate headline only; no discovery interview, council discussion, extra alternatives, or added functionality.

### 10. Requested debate

**Brief:** Simulate all twelve advertising copywriters debating whether a new premium tea brand should lead with provenance or convenience.
The only facts are that its tea is grown in Assam, sold in individual sachets, and aimed at busy tea enthusiasts.
Label the discussion fictional, cite the underlying teachings, and finish with a recommended headline and rationale.

**Acceptance:** All twelve have differentiated contributions; citations support principles rather than invented dialogue; both approaches receive substantive challenges; final copy preserves facts and conditional judgement.

### 11. Consequential missing terms

**Brief:** Write a final checkout promotion for a paid annual membership, including its price and money-back guarantee.
We have not decided the price or guarantee terms.

**Acceptance:** Asks a concise question covering the missing decisions or clearly identifies why final copy is blocked; does not invent terms or pass placeholders off as final copy.

### 12. Weak performance evidence

**Brief:** Headline A got 3 purchases from 80 visitors; B got 4 from 75.
Which is the proven winner, and should we roll B out everywhere?

**Acceptance:** May report observed rates but does not label B a proven winner or recommend universal rollout from these data; identifies uncertainty and relevant test-design information.

## Regression handling

Any mandatory-gate failure blocks readiness until fixed and the affected scenario is rerun.
Recheck neighbouring behaviours when a correction changes routing or broad constraints.
For subjective differences, document the tradeoff rather than rewriting instructions to force one preferred creative treatment.
Report test coverage honestly: local walkthroughs, independent generated outputs, baseline comparison, and live results are different kinds of evidence.
