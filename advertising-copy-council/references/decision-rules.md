# Decision rules

Use for uncertain strategy, conflicting council advice, or a mismatch between audience, message, and offer.
These rules are modern operational synthesis; historical sources are in [council-principles.md](council-principles.md).

## Priority

Factual integrity and the user's actual objective and constraints govern all creative choices.
If a requested claim is unsupported, explain the specific issue and produce the nearest defensible copy where possible.
Next consider the buyer's situation, available evidence, and medium.
Use historical principles as context-sensitive guidance within those boundaries.
Do not count votes or calculate a pseudo-scientific council score.

## Diagnose awareness separately from sophistication

Use this shorthand to choose a plausible starting point, not to force every reader through five steps:

| What the reader understands | Likely opening task |
|---|---|
| No active recognition of the need | Establish a relevant situation, desire, or observation without inventing a crisis. |
| Recognises the problem or desire | Clarify the problem and introduce a credible path toward improvement. |
| Understands possible solutions | Explain why this approach or category deserves consideration. |
| Knows the product but remains undecided | Address the important difference, evidence, objection, or fit. |
| Knows and wants the offer | Make availability, terms, and the next action easy to grasp. |

A mixed audience may need a broad entry point with layered detail rather than one rigid label.
Channel alone does not prove readiness.
For example, search can express early investigation or immediate purchase intent.

Sophistication concerns prior exposure to competing promises and explanations.
Ask what the market has heard, tried, and ceased to believe.
Where a familiar promise is doubted, consider better evidence, a more intelligible explanation, a narrower benefit, or a relevant identity and use context.
Do not invent novelty, give an ordinary process a misleading proprietary name, or increase claim magnitude merely to sound fresh.

## Resolve common tensions

| Tension | Decision |
|---|---|
| Response and brand building | Follow the objective and time horizon; make response work recognisably belong to the brand and brand work have an evaluable purpose. |
| Curiosity and clarity | Use curiosity if its payoff advances the relevant benefit; disclose essential offer information where the decision requires it. |
| Emotion and explanation | Use the personal stakes that fit the audience, backed by the information needed to judge the offer. |
| Long and short | Supply enough for the next decision within the medium; do not equate completeness with exhaustive length. |
| Features and benefits | Explain the practical consequence and retain technical specifications needed for fit or credibility. |
| Repetition and novelty | Preserve a useful central idea while varying expression when warranted; do not discard recognition simply because the writer is bored. |
| Unique and similar products | Investigate service, delivery, experience, offer, audience fit, or brand meaning; do not fabricate exclusive capabilities. |
| Story and direct offer | Start where the reader's attention and understanding justify it; omit a story that delays a purchase-ready decision without adding value. |
| Participation and direct purchase | Invite involvement when it serves the brief; do not add quizzes, contests, or extra steps by default. |
| Urgency and autonomy | Explain real timing consequences or supplied deadlines; omit invented pressure and resetting countdowns. |

## Evidence and missing facts

A demonstration supports what it actually demonstrates under its conditions.
A plausible mechanism explains how something could work but does not prove it works.
A customer account supports that person's reported experience and is not automatically representative.
Match the scope and strength of a claim to its evidence; retain material limitations and avoid implying unsupported typical results.

Choose a response proportional to the gap:

- Missing minor stylistic preference: choose a suitable voice from the brief.
- Missing price when price is unnecessary to the requested headline: omit it and complete the headline.
- Missing price or offer terms required for a purchase page: ask for the consequential facts or deliver clearly labelled partial work if requested.
- Unsupported result promise: use an evidenced capability or narrower benefit instead.
- Missing competitor verification: avoid comparative absolutes and explain the offer on its own merits.

Keep proposed guarantees, bonuses, pricing changes, and new capabilities outside finished copy until authorised and factually established.

## Evaluation

Define the hypothesis, intended audience, changed element, primary outcome, and important downstream checks when a test plan is requested.
For a copy comparison, hold unrelated elements reasonably stable or make the combined treatment explicit.
Use suitable assignment and sufficient evidence rather than treating before-and-after changes as automatically causal.
An A/B comparison of copy can estimate relative effects under its design; proving lift over no advertising requires an appropriate control.
Clicks, leads, purchases, retention, and brand measures answer different questions.
Report uncertainty and selection effects where material.
Do not infer statistical significance, required sample size, or business profitability without the necessary data and valid analysis.
