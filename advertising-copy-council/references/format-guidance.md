# Format guidance

Read only sections relevant to the assignment.
Use supplied limits; verify current platform specifications only when they are needed and not supplied.
Do not impose a default platform limit, language, or locale.
For unfamiliar formats, derive the structure from the reader's available attention, information needs, channel behaviour, and next action.

## Search and other constrained text ads

Match the query or placement's likely intent, keeping audience diagnoses provisional.
Prioritise the relevant benefit, product or offer identification, and a suitable action.
Count characters including spaces and punctuation under the platform's actual rules when relevant.
In multi-asset formats, avoid combinations that contradict one another or require a specific display order unless the configuration guarantees it.
Return the requested number of assets and include counts only when useful or requested.

## Social, display, outdoor, and print

Connect the opening and proposed visual to one recognisable idea.
On brief exposures, favour a quickly understood message and identifiable brand over a compressed sales letter.
Provide headline, body, CTA, or visual direction only to the extent requested or required by the format.
Match the ad's expectation to the destination.
For print or outdoor response details, use supplied URLs, addresses, phone numbers, and QR destinations; never invent them.

## Landing pages and sales letters

Match the entry promise and buyer readiness.
Develop enough of the promise, explanation, proof, objections, offer, and next step for the actual commitment being requested.
Use scannable structure and detail at the point where it helps a decision.
A lead-generation page, paid purchase page, and educational destination have different information burdens.
Do not fabricate testimonials or numerical proof to fill a familiar template.
For long letters, remove repetition that does not resolve a new doubt or reinforce an important decision.

## Product pages and technical B2B

Explain what the product is, who it fits, useful capabilities, limitations, and the next step.
Technical readers may need specifications, compatibility, operating conditions, or integration requirements before believing a benefit.
Retain exact supplied technical values and qualification language.
For example, "runs on Windows 11" does not establish that Windows 11 is required or that no other system is supported.
Distinguish a capability from a promised business result.
Do not invent certifications, integrations, security assurances, service levels, or installation requirements.

## Promotional emails and sequences

Make the subject and preview accurately lead into the body.
A subject line is not permission to invent an existing relationship, reply chain, account alert, or urgent event.
Use the supplied sender identity and customer relationship.
Choose one main job for each message unless the user's format calls for several.
For sequences, give each email a useful role, such as explanation, demonstration, objection handling, or a real deadline reminder.
Avoid repeating the same pitch with cosmetic edits.
Preserve supplied dates and terms consistently across messages.

## Video, audio, and spoken advertising

Write words people can comfortably say and hear.
For video, distinguish spoken copy, on-screen text, and visual direction when useful.
For audio, ensure essential meaning survives without visuals.
Use a timing budget that includes pauses, demonstrations, and the required CTA.
Estimate spoken duration from an explicit pace assumption and verify by a timed read where available; label estimates honestly.
Do not claim a script is exactly timed from word count alone.
Avoid visuals that imply fabricated before-and-after evidence or undisclosed results.

## Brand, luxury, and awareness work

Follow the brand's actual identity and campaign objective.
A restrained promise, memorable image, or product experience may be more appropriate than an aggressive offer.
Do not add discounts, artificial scarcity, lead magnets, or hard-sell CTAs against the brief.
Brand meaning must still attach clearly to the advertised brand or offer.

## Critiques and variants

Critique the submitted words and their relation to the brief rather than reciting general principles.
Prioritise issues that affect truth, comprehension, relevance, differentiation, or action.
Keep changes within the requested scope.
If a variant set is intended to explore strategy, vary a meaningful premise; if the user requests stylistic variants, preserve the premise and facts.
