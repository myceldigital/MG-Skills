# Council principles and sources

Read relevant records for deeper judgement, attribution, or a requested debate.
Sources were inspected during the originating research on 2026-09-07.
Some links are reproductions or indexed excerpts; verify an accessible edition before quoting exact wording or making stronger historical claims.
These concise paraphrases are not complete accounts of each writer's work.
Published advocacy establishes what was taught; campaign anecdotes do not establish universal effectiveness.
Each Teaching is attributed to its source; Application and Limit are this skill's synthesis.

## Claude C. Hopkins

**Teaching:** Treat advertising as selling, use traceable responses and test campaigns, prefer definite statements, and provide interested readers with a sufficient case.

**Application:** Replace empty superiority with supported details and connect evaluation to customer acquisition or the actual commercial objective.

**Limit:** Historical certainty about advertising laws is not modern causal evidence; a short ad need not contain an entire sales argument.

**Source:** [Scientific Advertising, chapters 1-4, 7-8, and 15](https://draytonbird.net/hopkins.pdf), reproduced original text.

## David Ogilvy

**Teaching:** Advertising contributes to long-term brand personality; define the intended identity and maintain coherence.
Research the product to find useful creative material.

**Application:** Develop a memorable idea from a factual foundation while preserving the user's voice and positioning.

**Limit:** Brand consistency cannot excuse an unclear offer; an agency retrospective is not an independently controlled sales study.

**Sources:** [The Image and the Brand, 1955 speech](https://www.markenlexikon.com/texte/ogilvy_the_image_of_the_brand_1955.pdf), reproduced by courtesy of Ogilvy & Mather; [agency account of Rolls-Royce research](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/ai-end-road-ad-business).
Use the latter for its research anecdote rather than its broader AI opinions or causal sales assertions.

## Howard Gossage

**Teaching:** Advertising competes with everything else that interests readers; his work used reader engagement, participation, and ideas extending into publicity.

**Application:** Identify what the audience gets from the encounter and whether an invitation or demonstration serves the purpose.

**Limit:** Participation is optional; do not force a contest into purchase-ready copy or claim to know what Gossage would endorse today.

**Source:** [Interview with biographer Steve Harrison](https://www.adpulp.com/the-adpulp-interview-steve-harrison-howard-gossages-biographer/), secondary testimony discussing original work.

## Gary Halbert

**Teaching:** The starving-crowd exercise prioritises hungry buyers; purchasing behaviour can indicate market appetite.

**Application:** Investigate active demand, prior attempts, and purchase triggers before polishing the pitch.

**Limit:** Category demand does not establish preference for this offer; missing purchase data means uncertainty, not proof of no demand.

**Source:** [Direct Marketing to a Starving Crowd](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/direct_marketing_to_a_starving_crowd.htm), Halbert newsletter archive.

## Joe Sugarman

**Teaching:** The slippery-slide concept connects the opening and subsequent sentences to maintain reading through the selling argument.

**Application:** Repair heavy openings, abrupt transitions, digressions, and suspense without a payoff.

**Limit:** Momentum cannot substitute for evidence or justify burying essential terms; allow scanning when that better serves the decision.

**Source:** [The Adweek Copywriting Handbook, chapter 8](https://fliphtml5.com/vuack/pyou), third-party reproduction of Sugarman's text.

## Eugene Schwartz

**Teaching:** Channel existing mass desire and adapt to awareness and exposure to competing claims.

**Application:** Diagnose what the audience knows separately from what it doubts; choose the opening and evidence accordingly.

**Limit:** Common five-level labels are modern shorthand rather than a compulsory linear funnel.
Do not address scepticism by inventing a mechanism or escalating unsupported promises.

**Sources:** [Authorised publisher's system explanation](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/lp4-breakthrough-advertising-system/); [publisher's modern awareness explainer](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/the-five-states-of-awareness-the-key-to-marketing-that-actually-works/).
These are explanations of the book, not transcripts of Schwartz or independent validation of guaranteed results.

## Leo Burnett

**Teaching:** Discover an honest, engaging expression of inherent product drama through words, images, simplicity, and emotional reward.

**Application:** Find a revealing use moment, product detail, experience, or demonstration that makes the value tangible.

**Limit:** Drama must connect to the product; an invented scene is not a documented customer result.

**Source:** [Finally Somebody Has to Get Out an Ad](https://medium.com/leos-words-of-wisdom/finally-somebody-has-to-get-out-an-ad-f558baf9141), reproduced Burnett essay.

## John Caples

**Teaching:** Emphasise reader benefit and news in headlines, be cautious with curiosity alone, and compare approaches through response.

**Application:** Develop meaningfully different openings whose expectations the body fulfils.

**Limit:** Historical headline rankings are hypotheses for new contexts; attention alone is not sales impact.

**Sources:** [Tested Advertising Methods, reproduced text](https://www.scribd.com/document/830542329/John-Caples-Tested-Advertising-Methods); [book introduction and description](https://www.scientificadvertising.com/books/tam/).
Revised editions differ; verify edition and passage for exact quotation or page references.

## Rosser Reeves

**Teaching:** A USP offers a specific benefit, something competitors cannot or do not offer, and enough importance to attract customers.

**Application:** Separate the promise from the reason to prefer this offer and assess whether the difference matters.

**Limit:** Being first to explain a shared feature does not make it exclusive; unusual attributes have no automatic commercial value.

**Sources:** [Reality in Advertising, indexed USP passage](https://ychoc.com/seo-marketing/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ychoc-com-reality-in-advertising-Rosser-Reeves.pdf); [Bly quoting the three requirements](https://www.bly.com/blog/general/the-3-part-formula-for-a-winning-usp/).
The original PDF passage was inspected through indexed excerpts; direct retrieval was unreliable.

## Bob Bly

**Teaching:** Link features to benefits; specialists may need substantial feature detail, which explains the basis of a benefit.

**Application:** State the capability and practical consequence while retaining information needed for expert evaluation.

**Limit:** Explanation does not independently validate performance; do not replace precision with vague simplicity.

**Source:** [Take Your Copywriting to the Next Level, benefits discussion](https://www.bly.com/reportsbonus/TakeYourCopywritingToTheNextLevel.pdf), author's report inspected through indexed excerpts; direct retrieval was unreliable.

## Dan Kennedy

**Teaching:** Align message, market, and media around the intended customer and how they receive and respond to information.

**Application:** Fit the offer and next action to that audience and medium; make the exchange understandable.

**Limit:** Do not universalise a direct-response preference into a ban on brand work or treat a client anecdote as independent causal evidence.

**Source:** [The 3 Ms of Successful Direct Marketing Campaigns](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/the-3-ms-of-successful-direct-marketing-campaigns-new/313518), Kennedy-authored book excerpt.

## John Carlton

**Teaching:** A hook connects tangible product qualities to inner needs, including believability; finding it can require deeper, repeated inquiry.

**Application:** Investigate unexpected details, customer language, failed attempts, and overlooked use cases.

**Limit:** Borrow the investigative discipline without automatically copying an abrasive voice or demanding sensationalism.

**Source:** [Fishing for Hooks](https://www.john-carlton.com/2006/10/fishing-for-hooks/), Carlton's own article.

## Modern measurement qualification

Observational attribution and experimental effects can differ substantially.
Even large advertising experiments may leave wide uncertainty about returns.
Treat proposed copy improvements as hypotheses until adequate evidence exists.

**Sources:** [Gordon et al., 2019, Marketing Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mksc.2018.1135); [Lewis and Rao, 2015, Quarterly Journal of Economics](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/130/4/1941/1914592), primary research.
