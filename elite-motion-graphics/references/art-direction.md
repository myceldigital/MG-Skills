# Art direction and visual authorship

## Begin with the communication problem

Write a single sentence connecting the viewer, tension, transformation, and takeaway.
For example: a founder drowning in disconnected updates sees separate fragments become one legible decision.
The mechanism of the animation should express that transformation.
A collection of stylish effects with interchangeable copy is an execution exercise, not a developed concept.

Identify the film's dominant mode: persuasion, explanation, identity, spectacle, emotion, or narrative.
These modes can coexist, but one should govern the viewer's attention.
Choose an ending early: resolve the opening question, reveal a useful contrast, land the identity, or return to the beginning with new meaning.
If the brief contains several messages, rank them before adding scenes.

## Construct distinct concept routes

Each route needs a premise, a repeatable graphic mechanism, a hero frame, a temporal idea, a sonic idea, and a risk.
Change the concept, not just the palette.

| Route mechanism | Useful message | Motion implication | Risk to control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fragments become a system | Clarity, coordination, synthesis | Separate trajectories resolve into one meaningful arrangement | Confusing complexity before the viewer understands the problem |
| One object changes its role | Adaptability, product breadth | Preserve identity through changes in scale, material, and function | Morphs become arbitrary and information arrives too quickly |
| A hidden structure is revealed | Discovery, precision, intelligence | Occlusion, sectioning, negative space, selective light | Decorative mystery delays understanding |
| A physical rule changes | Breakthrough, freedom, play | Establish the rule, violate it once, show its consequence | Constant rule changes remove the surprise |
| Scale changes meaning | Reach, detail, interconnectedness | A detail becomes a scene, a scene becomes a larger object | Zooms hide weak staging or unreadable detail |
| A repeated action evolves | Progress, craft, mastery | Repetition establishes expectation; one variation creates payoff | Mechanical repetition without escalation |

Select against message fit, recognizability, distinction from category habits, consistency across shots, and feasibility in the actual time budget.
Develop one primary device and a small supporting vocabulary.
Use unexpected contrast inside a coherent system, not constant novelty.

## Analyze references without becoming derivative

For every reference, distinguish directly observed properties from inferred intent.
Measure representative shot lengths; inspect entrances, reading holds, exits, and transition midpoints.
Sample palette roles instead of copying every pixel color.
Name the motion grammar: what accelerates, what stays still, which axis dominates, and what survives a cut.
Map the density and energy curve across the film.
Compare at least one quiet interval with the strongest interval when the reference permits it.

Borrow an abstract principle, such as typography behaving as architecture, and translate it into the user's subject.
Do not copy a distinctive sequence, character, logo, composition series, or branded assets as a substitute for authorship.
A named aesthetic is shorthand; extract its concrete properties and adapt them.
Record the provenance and usage permission of assets actually incorporated.

## Build a compact visual system

Define each token by role and behavior:

- Color: ground, primary information, supporting information, action accent, semantic states.
- Type: display, explanatory copy, data/UI, captions; actual families, weights, sizes, line heights, tracking, and fallback strategy.
- Geometry: grid, spacing steps, corner radii, stroke widths, optical corrections, alignments.
- Motion: durations or physical parameters for each class of object, sequencing offsets, settle behavior, allowed overshoot.
- Depth: layer hierarchy, shadow/light model, perspective, parallax, and occlusion rules.
- Texture: material, scale, contrast, temporal behavior, and regions where it must not impair text.
- Sound: instrument/material family, transient character, register, density, and silence.

A restricted palette is a useful starting constraint, not a definition of sophistication.
One display family and one supporting family are often enough; more require a clear role.
Optical balance can require offsets from a mathematical center.
Keep stroke weight and texture scale coherent under camera changes.

## Compose for attention

Give each moment one primary focus, a subordinate context, and a resting area.
Use scale, contrast, motion onset, isolation, directional lines, and occlusion deliberately.
Moving elements attract attention, so keep secondary activity quieter during a key reading hold.
Carry the viewer's eye between shots by matching position, direction, scale, or shape when continuity matters.
Break that continuity intentionally when a reset or shock serves the film.

Inspect silhouettes and value structure before adding effects.
If a focal object is weak without bloom, noise, shadow, or chromatic aberration, repair its composition first.
Depth can come from overlap, value separation, scale, or timing; it does not require a 3D engine.
Design the negative space occupied by a future element before it arrives.

## Typography as temporal composition

Treat words as meaning before treating letters as shapes.
Choose line breaks by thought, emphasis, and visual rhythm.
Animate semantic groups unless letter-level action expresses a specific idea.
Preserve kerning, baselines, punctuation, diacritics, ligatures, and script direction.
Use grapheme segmentation for typewriter effects; slicing code units can break emoji and combined characters.

Define separate entry, readable hold, and exit windows.
Reading time starts once the text is stable and legible, not when its first glyph begins to appear.
Test exact copy at the intended viewing size, including the longest localized variant if localization is in scope.
If it cannot be read, shorten the copy, increase its prominence, simplify the surrounding action, or extend the hold.
Do not solve overcrowding only by shrinking text.

For type masks, inspect the first and last visible strokes, ascenders, descenders, accents, and antialiasing at fractional positions.
For large type, check spacing optically instead of relying on default tracking.
Render at target resolution and inspect after delivery compression.

## Avoid generic output through decisions

Centered headlines, corner labels, gradients, particles, glass, and glow are not intrinsically wrong.
They become weak when they appear because no conceptual decision was made.
Ask what the effect means, which information it supports, and whether removing it improves the scene.
Do not add noise to every frame to simulate craft.
Do not make every shot a different aesthetic to simulate range.

A memorable reel demonstrates range inside authorship: vary scale, density, material, and pacing while retaining a recognizable graphic logic.
A luxury film may need a long hold and one exceptional material transition.
An explainer may need an unglamorous but exact diagram.
Judge the work against its purpose.
