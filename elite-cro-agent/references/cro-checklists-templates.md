# CRO Checklists And Templates

Use this reference for page reviews, copy rewrites, experiment plans, offer redesign, and structured outputs.

## Landing Page Review Checklist

### First Screen

- Is the target customer obvious?
- Is the main outcome concrete?
- Is the differentiator visible?
- Is the CTA clear?
- Is there immediate credibility?
- Does the page continue the traffic-source promise?
- Is there a reason to keep reading?

### Copy

- Is the headline specific?
- Does the subheadline clarify the promise?
- Are benefits connected to outcomes?
- Are features translated into customer value?
- Is copy written in customer language?
- Are objections handled directly?
- Are claims supported by proof?
- Is the tone appropriate for the buyer and category?

### Offer

- Is the offer easy to understand?
- Is the next step proportional to visitor readiness?
- Is risk reversed or reduced?
- Is pricing framed clearly?
- Is urgency legitimate?
- Is the offer differentiated from alternatives?
- Does the offer attract the right customer?

### Proof

- Are proof points specific?
- Are testimonials credible and outcome-oriented?
- Are logos relevant to the target segment?
- Are case studies close to the CTA or claim they support?
- Are numbers contextualised?
- Is proof matched to objections?

### UX And Flow

- Is the page easy to scan?
- Does the visual hierarchy match the argument hierarchy?
- Are forms as short as appropriate?
- Are required fields justified by value or qualification?
- Is mobile experience strong?
- Are CTAs repeated at natural decision points?
- Are distractions removed near the primary action?

### Conversion Quality

- Could the page create bad-fit conversions?
- Is qualification sufficient?
- Are expectations accurate?
- Does the page overpromise?
- Are downstream teams likely to receive better or worse leads?

## Copywriting Patterns

Prefer these patterns when rewriting conversion copy.

### Outcome Headline

```markdown
Get [specific outcome] without [major pain or tradeoff].
```

### Segment-Specific Headline

```markdown
For [specific audience] who need to [specific job], [product] helps you [specific outcome].
```

### Mechanism Headline

```markdown
The [category/product] that helps [audience] achieve [outcome] by [distinct mechanism].
```

### Risk-Reversal CTA Support

```markdown
Start with [low-risk step]. No [common fear]. You can [exit/control/reverse] anytime.
```

### Proof-Driven Claim

```markdown
[Specific customer/segment] achieved [specific result] in [timeframe] using [mechanism].
```

Avoid:

- "Revolutionary"
- "Seamless"
- "Next-generation"
- "All-in-one" without specifics.
- "Unlock your potential."
- "Transform your business" without proof.
- Generic "save time and money."

## Experiment Design Standards

Do not propose weak tests such as "test a new headline" without a reason.

Every experiment must define:

- The diagnosed barrier.
- The visitor segment.
- The psychological or commercial mechanism.
- The exact page area or funnel step.
- The expected behavioral change.
- The primary success metric.
- Guardrail metrics.
- Minimum data requirements or caveats.
- What to do if the test wins, loses, or is inconclusive.

Interpretation rules:

- A win without a plausible mechanism is fragile.
- A loss can still be valuable if it invalidates a meaningful hypothesis.
- An inconclusive test may mean the change was too weak, traffic was insufficient, or the constraint was misdiagnosed.
- Do not stop tests early because the early trend is convenient.
- Always check downstream quality before declaring victory.

## Prioritisation Framework

Use this scoring model when prioritisation is requested:

```markdown
Impact: 1-5
Confidence: 1-5
Ease: 1-5
Downstream risk: 1-5, where 5 is high risk
Learning value: 1-5
Priority score = Impact + Confidence + Ease + Learning value - Downstream risk
```

High-risk changes require stronger measurement and clearer guardrails.
