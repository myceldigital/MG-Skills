# CRO Diagnostic Model

Use this reference for full audits, strategic CRO diagnosis, and deep funnel reviews.

## Diagnostic Lenses

Assess conversion through ten lenses. Do not treat them as equal. Identify the dominant constraint.

## 1. Visitor Intent

Ask:

- Why is this person here now?
- What triggered their search or click?
- Are they problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or ready to buy?
- What does the traffic source promise before the page loads?
- Is the page continuing that promise clearly?

Common failure:

- The page answers a different question than the visitor brought with them.

Elite fix:

- Align the first screen, headline, proof, CTA, and offer with the visitor's current intent.

## 2. Offer Architecture

The offer is not just the product and price. It includes:

- Promise.
- Desired outcome.
- Mechanism.
- Package.
- Price.
- Terms.
- Risk reversal.
- Bonuses or added value.
- Urgency.
- Scarcity.
- Social proof.
- Implementation support.
- Next step.

Ask:

- What deal does the customer believe they are accepting?
- Is the outcome worth the money, time, risk, and effort?
- What makes this offer meaningfully different?
- Does the offer attract high-quality customers or only easy conversions?
- Is the next step too large for the visitor's readiness?

Common failure:

- The business tries to fix a weak offer with copy, layout, or button changes.

Elite fix:

- Strengthen the perceived deal before polishing the interface.

## 3. Message Hierarchy

The page must build belief in the right order.

Recommended sequence:

1. Relevance: "This is for me."
2. Value: "This solves a problem I care about."
3. Differentiation: "This is not just another generic option."
4. Mechanism: "I understand how it works."
5. Proof: "I believe it can work."
6. Fit: "It can work for someone like me."
7. Risk reduction: "Trying this feels safe enough."
8. Action: "The next step is clear and reasonable."

Ask:

- Does the first screen earn continued attention?
- Are true claims presented in the right order?
- Is the page selling the outcome before explaining the machinery?
- Does each section resolve a real question or hesitation?

Common failure:

- The page lists features before establishing why the visitor should care.

Elite fix:

- Reorder the page around the buyer's belief journey.

## 4. Clarity

Clarity is the first conversion multiplier.

Check whether the visitor can quickly answer:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What outcome does it create?
- Why is it better or different?
- What must I do next?
- What happens after I click?

Common failure:

- The page uses internal language, category jargon, vague adjectives, or clever copy at the expense of comprehension.

Elite fix:

- Replace abstraction with concrete outcomes, plain language, and specific next steps.

## 5. Proof And Believability

Proof must match the objection.

Use:

- Customer proof for "do people like me use this?"
- Outcome proof for "does this work?"
- Data proof for "how much impact can I expect?"
- Demonstration proof for "can I see it?"
- Process proof for "how does this happen?"
- Security proof for "is this safe?"
- Expert proof for "is this credible?"
- Comparison proof for "why this instead of alternatives?"
- Operational proof for "can they actually deliver?"

Ask:

- What is the biggest claim?
- What would a skeptical buyer need to see to believe it?
- Is the proof specific, relevant, recent, and close to the claim?
- Are testimonials generic or tied to concrete outcomes?

Common failure:

- The page adds logos and testimonials without resolving the actual objection.

Elite fix:

- Build a proof system that answers the visitor's real doubts at the moment those doubts appear.

## 6. Friction, Anxiety, And Inertia

Separate friction types:

- Cognitive friction: "I do not understand."
- Effort friction: "This looks like work."
- Emotional friction: "I do not feel safe."
- Trust friction: "I do not believe you."
- Value friction: "This may not be worth it."
- Timing friction: "Maybe later."
- Identity friction: "This may not be for people like me."
- Commitment friction: "I do not want to be trapped."

Ask:

- Which friction is useful qualification?
- Which friction is needless resistance?
- What anxiety appears right before the CTA?
- What fear is unspoken but active?

Common failure:

- Teams remove all friction and accidentally increase low-quality leads, refunds, churn, or sales waste.

Elite fix:

- Remove friction that blocks qualified action. Preserve friction that improves fit, seriousness, or economics.

## 7. CTA And Commitment Fit

The CTA is the commitment being requested.

Assess:

- Is the CTA too high commitment for cold traffic?
- Is it too low commitment for high-intent traffic?
- Does the CTA text describe value or only mechanics?
- Is there a lower-risk secondary action when appropriate?
- Does the visitor know what happens after clicking?

CTA examples by commitment:

- Low: "See examples", "Calculate savings", "Watch demo", "Get the guide".
- Medium: "Start free trial", "Create account", "Build my plan".
- High: "Book demo", "Talk to sales", "Buy now", "Request proposal".

Common failure:

- Asking cold, uncertain visitors for a sales conversation before they understand value.

Elite fix:

- Match CTA commitment to awareness, intent, price, complexity, and risk.

## 8. Segmentation And Message Match

Adapt the page when decision criteria differ meaningfully.

Segment by:

- Traffic source.
- Search intent.
- Buyer role.
- Industry.
- Company size.
- Use case.
- Awareness level.
- Sophistication.
- Lifecycle stage.
- Purchase urgency.

Ask:

- Are materially different visitors being forced through one generic argument?
- Does ad or email language continue on the page?
- Do different segments need different proof, CTA, or objections handled?

Common failure:

- Superficial personalization that changes labels but not the argument.

Elite fix:

- Personalize the decision logic, not just the salutation.

## 9. Design Trust And Visual Hierarchy

Design is trust before words.

Assess:

- Does the page feel credible for the price and category?
- Does visual hierarchy direct attention to the right sequence?
- Are primary and secondary CTAs visually distinct?
- Are proof, pricing, and guarantees easy to find?
- Does the design reduce cognitive load or create it?
- Does the page feel premium, cheap, chaotic, generic, or trustworthy?

Common failure:

- Beautiful design that hides the argument or direct-response design that damages trust.

Elite fix:

- Use design to create comprehension, confidence, and desire.

## 10. Economics And Downstream Quality

Never judge conversion rate alone.

Track:

- Qualified conversion rate.
- Revenue per visitor.
- Average order value.
- Lead-to-opportunity rate.
- Opportunity-to-close rate.
- CAC payback.
- Activation rate.
- Refund rate.
- Churn.
- Retention.
- LTV.
- Support burden.
- No-show rate.
- Sales cycle length.

Ask:

- Could this change increase conversions while damaging business quality?
- Which metric best represents a valuable conversion?
- What guardrail metrics must not deteriorate?

Common failure:

- Celebrating more leads while sales quality collapses.

Elite fix:

- Optimise for profitable, qualified commitment.

## Constraint Categories

Use these categories when summarising a diagnosis:

- Demand problem: The audience is not sufficiently motivated.
- Audience mismatch: The page is attracting or addressing the wrong visitors.
- Offer weakness: The perceived deal is not compelling.
- Message mismatch: The page breaks the promise made by the ad, email, search result, or referral.
- Clarity problem: Visitors cannot understand value quickly enough.
- Proof deficit: Claims are not believable enough.
- Trust deficit: Visitors doubt the company, product, category, data handling, or delivery.
- Friction problem: The action requires too much work or attention.
- Anxiety problem: Visitors fear loss, embarrassment, lock-in, poor fit, hidden costs, or failure.
- CTA mismatch: The requested commitment does not match readiness.
- Pricing/value problem: Price is not justified by perceived value.
- Design hierarchy problem: The visual structure does not support the decision sequence.
- Downstream quality problem: The page converts people the business should not want.
